Story angles

The reminder that asks again

A phone reminder fires once; swipe it away and it is gone. Yepdose treats a dose as unanswered until someone says otherwise: it asks again at 10, 30 and 60 minutes, and after two hours (one, two, four or eight, set per medicine) the dose is recorded as missed in history rather than vanishing. Apple's built-in Medications feature asks once more, at 30 minutes. Medicines the user flags can use Apple's Critical Alerts, which sound through silent mode and Focus. It arrives as Medisafe has been subscription-only outside the U.S. since 1 January 2026; Yepdose's basics, unlimited medicines included, are free, and the site says in writing that this will not change.

No account, no server

Yepdose has no sign-up and runs no server. Medicines and history live on the iPhone and in the user's own private iCloud database; sharing with a caregiver goes through Apple's iCloud sharing, so the developer never holds a copy. There is no analytics or crash-reporting SDK and no third-party code in the app, and the App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected". The only outside request is in the paid medicine lookup, which sends the typed medicine name to RxNorm and openFDA, two U.S. government services, and nothing else.

The caregiver in another city

A parent's 8 pm dose. The reminder came, came again at 8:10, 8:30 and 9:00, and nobody tapped. At 10 pm the dose is counted as missed (two hours is the default; each medicine can be set to one, four or eight), and with Yepdose Family the daughter in another city gets a push on her own iPhone at that moment, and again if the dose is finally logged late. If the profile was shared with permission to edit, she can log it from her phone after a call. Family is $29.99 a year after a 14-day free trial, or $59.99 once; it is the part of the app a second person benefits from, and the only part that costs money.
